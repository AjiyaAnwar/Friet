What changed in the merge

Team 1's original MVP ERD (19 entities) and Team 2's Commercial Domain ERD covered overlapping ground for the "front half" of the business (customer, carrier, vendor, RFQ, quotation) but at very different levels of detail. This version keeps one definition per concept, split back into five diagrams so each one is actually legible — the same format Team 2's PDF used, just extended to cover Team 1's pieces too.

Entities replaced (Team 2's richer version kept, Team 1's simpler duplicate dropped)

Entities kept from Team 1 as-is (Diagram 5 — Ops-owned; Team 2 explicitly did not model these)

SHIPMENT, SHIPMENT_LEG, CONTAINER, CARGO, PACKAGE, TRACKING_EVENT, DOCUMENT, REVENUE_LINE, COST_LINE, INVOICE. Team 2's Diagram 4 drew Shipment only as a one-field skeleton with the note "full model owned by Ops workstream, not Team 2" — this merge fills that skeleton in with Team 1's original fields rather than inventing a second, competing definition.

Their loose text fields were tightened to point at Team 2's new master data instead of staying free text, since that data now exists: Container.container_type_code → ContainerType.code, Document.doc_type_code → DocumentType.code, RevenueLine.charge_code / CostLine.charge_code → ChargeCode.code.

Entities kept exactly as Team 2 defined them

Diagram 1 (Master & Reference Data), Diagram 3 (Rate/Tariff Engine), and the rest of Diagram 4 (Route, RouteLeg) — no Team 1 equivalent existed, so nothing to reconcile.

Still an open question (not resolved by this merge)

  • MarginRule — kept as its own table here, matching the current standalone implementation, but Team 2 flagged it as a candidate to instead live as rows in Team 1's generic rule table (domain = margin_rules). Needs Team 1's call before migration.
  • Base entity convention — Team 1's own spec calls for every table to carry id, created_at, updated_at, created_by, tenant_id. Those are omitted from the diagrams above for readability (same as both source documents did) but should be assumed on every table.
  • RLS — every tenant-scoped table (Customer, RFQ, Quotation, Job, Shipment, etc.) should carry tenant_id and the same row-level-security policy, per Team 1's §1.2. Not yet drawn per-table above.
  • Route vs Shipment Leg — RouteLeg (Diagram 4, planning-stage: what a quotation option proposes) and ShipmentLeg (Diagram 5, execution-stage: what actually happened) are kept as two distinct tables on purpose, not an accidental duplicate — worth a one-line confirmation from both teams.

Priority-list mapping (from Team 1's entity priority doc)

The "must-have first" / "can defer" split from Team 1's priority list still applies to the merged model — it just now maps onto the richer entities: the deferred CARRIER/VENDOR/CONTAINER/CARGO/PACKAGE/COST_LINE are still fine to build later, and everything new from Team 2 (master data, rate engine, RFQ/quotation detail tables) sits behind the same must-have anchors (RFQ, QUOTATION, JOB→SHIPMENT) it always did.
